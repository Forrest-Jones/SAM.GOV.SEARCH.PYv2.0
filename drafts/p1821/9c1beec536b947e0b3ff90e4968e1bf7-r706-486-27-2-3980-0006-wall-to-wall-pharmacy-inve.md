The requirement consists of a base period plus four option periods. All physical inventories at the six facilities shall be completed no later than February 28 of the applicable contract year. 1. PURPOSE
The Department of Veterans Affairs (VA), Veterans Integrated Service Network (VISN) 9, requires the services of a qualified contractor to conduct annual wall-to-wall physical inventories of pharmacy medications and applicable pharmacy supplies at designated VA Medical Centers.
The Contractor shall provide all labor, supervision, equipment, supplies, software or inventory tools, travel, and other resources necessary to perform the required services.
2. BACKGROUND
VISN 9 requires an annual physical inventory of pharmacy medications and applicable pharmacy supplies at the facilities identified in this Statement of Work (SOW).
The inventory shall support the VA's financial, pharmacy management, and inventory-control requirements by establishing an accurate record of inventory quantities and replacement costs.
The Contractor shall perform the inventory in a manner that minimizes disruption to pharmacy operations and patient-care activities.

5. ANNUAL INVENTORY REQUIREMENT
The Contractor shall perform one complete wall-to-wall inventory at each designated facility during each contract period.
Unless otherwise approved in advance by the Contracting Officer or authorized VA representative, the physical inventory at each facility shall:
Begin at 8:00 a.m. local time on the scheduled inventory date.
Be completed within one business day.
Be scheduled on a Saturday or Sunday, when operationally feasible, to minimize disruption to patient-care activities.
Include all applicable pharmacy areas and inventory categories identified in this SOW.
The Government will establish the final inventory schedule in coordination with the Contractor and affected VA Medical Center personnel.
Any deviation from the one-day requirement must be coordinated with the affected pharmacy and approved by the Contracting Officer or authorized representative.
The Contractor shall provide a database with accurately formatted National Drug Code (NDC) and McKesson item numbers that correspond to the applicable products.
All physical inventories at the six facilities shall be completed no later than February 28 of each applicable contract year.
6. CONTRACTOR QUALIFICATIONS
The Contractor shall demonstrate a minimum of three years of experience performing wall-to-wall pharmacy inventory services.
The Contractor shall also demonstrate experience involving pharmacy inventory operations, medication and pharmacy-supply identification, NDC identification and validation, replacement-cost determination, McKesson Prime Vendor data where applicable, and electronic inventory reporting.
Experience performing similar services for VA pharmacies and experience with McKesson as a Prime Vendor are preferred.
7. INVENTORY SCOPE
The Contractor shall inventory applicable medications and pharmacy supplies located within areas under the direct control of the pharmacy.
Unless specifically excluded by this SOW, the inventory shall include:
Products purchased through the McKesson Prime Vendor.
Eligible direct purchases having a total dollar value of $100 or more per line item.
Applicable prosthetic items in unopened containers having a cost exceeding $100.
Inpatient pharmacy inventory.
Outpatient pharmacy inventory.
Bulk intravenous solutions.
Unit-dose repackaged medications.
Medications located within applicable automated dispensing and packaging equipment.
Other medications and supplies meeting the definition of inventory under this SOW.
8. EXCLUDED ITEMS
The following items shall not be included unless specifically directed by the Government:
All Hazards Pharmaceutical Cache inventory.
Equipment.
Prescription labels.
Empty prescription vials and containers.
Items not under the direct control of the pharmacy.
Patient-dispensed inventory that is no longer pharmacy stock.
Controlled substances, which shall be physically counted by authorized VA Pharmacy personnel.
For purposes of determining whether an item is included or excluded, the Contractor shall request clarification from the designated VA Pharmacy representative. Questions affecting contract scope, price, or other contractual requirements shall be referred to the Contracting Officer.
9. INPATIENT PHARMACY INVENTORY
The Contractor shall inventory applicable items located within the inpatient pharmacy, including medication and inventory maintained within automated dispensing, packaging, or unit-dose equipment.
Applicable systems may include Talyst, Omnicell, AutoMed, ATC, OS-PAC, unit-dose packaging systems, and other automated dispensing or packaging systems identified by the facility.
The VA Pharmacy shall provide the Contractor with available inventory data from applicable automated systems. The Contractor shall incorporate the VA-provided automated-system data into the applicable inventory report.
10. OUTPATIENT PHARMACY INVENTORY
The Contractor shall inventory applicable items located within the outpatient pharmacy, including inventory maintained within automated dispensing or fulfillment equipment.
Applicable systems may include Baker APS, ScriptPro, Optifill, and other automated dispensing or fulfillment systems identified by the facility.
The VA Pharmacy shall provide available inventory data from applicable automated systems for incorporation into the final inventory report.
11. CONTROLLED SUBSTANCES
Controlled substances shall be physically counted by authorized VISN 9/VA Pharmacy personnel.
The VA Pharmacy shall provide the Contractor with the applicable controlled-substance inventory data necessary for inclusion in the final inventory report.
The Contractor shall not independently count, handle, move, reconcile, or otherwise perform a physical inventory of controlled substances unless specifically authorized by the Government in accordance with applicable VA requirements.
12. PARTIAL CONTAINERS
Opened bottles of medication, other than medication used for compounding, shall be estimated to the nearest one-tenth of a bottle unless otherwise specified by the Government.
For higher-cost medications where a more precise quantity can reasonably be established, the Contractor shall have a methodology capable of recording whole-unit quantities for partial containers.
The Contractor shall document the methodology used and apply it consistently throughout the inventory.
13. INVENTORY IDENTIFICATION AND SECTION CONTROL
The Contractor shall establish a method for identifying each inventory section before counting begins.
The method shall clearly identify the beginning and end of each section, identify the inventory specialist responsible for counting the section, prevent duplication or omission when multiple inventory specialists work simultaneously, allow VA personnel to determine which areas have been completed, and support final reconciliation of all inventory areas.
The Contractor shall remove all temporary inventory markers at the completion of the inventory after confirming that all applicable areas have been counted.
14. INVENTORY DATA AND PRODUCT IDENTIFICATION
The Contractor shall maintain an inventory database capable of identifying applicable products using accurate NDC, UPC, UPN, and McKesson item-number information, as applicable.
The Contractor shall use current and accurate product and pricing information to determine the replacement cost of inventoried items.
Where McKesson Prime Vendor information is used, the Contractor shall ensure that product identification corresponds to the applicable McKesson item and associated VA pricing information.
The Contractor shall not alter or manipulate VA-provided costing files for the purpose of correcting Government-provided data.
If the Contractor identifies an apparent discrepancy, it shall document the discrepancy and obtain clarification from the VA Pharmacy before making any adjustment attributable to Government-provided information.
Contractor-caused inventory errors shall be corrected at no additional cost to the Government.
15. VA-FURNISHED INFORMATION
The VA facility shall provide, as applicable:
Current Prime Vendor pricing information.
Current costing information for direct-purchase items.
Automated dispensing and packaging system data.
Controlled-substance inventory data.
Access to pharmacy areas necessary to perform the inventory.
Knowledgeable pharmacy personnel to answer inventory questions.
Current shelf labels for Prime Vendor products, to the extent available.
McKesson Mobile Manager units or equivalent Government-provided pricing information, when applicable.
Other information reasonably necessary to perform the requirement.
The Contractor shall promptly notify the VA Pharmacy representative of missing or apparently inaccurate information.
16. CONTRACTOR-FURNISHED RESOURCES
The Contractor shall furnish sufficient qualified personnel to complete each facility inventory within the required performance period.
The Contractor shall provide inventory specialists, on-site supervision, inventory equipment and supplies, data-processing and reporting capability, required travel, transportation, temporary identification or tagging materials, and all other resources necessary to perform the requirement.
17. ON-SITE INVENTORY PROCEDURE
The Contractor shall:
Meet with the designated VA Pharmacy representative before beginning the inventory.
Confirm the areas included in the inventory.
Confirm receipt of applicable VA-provided data.
Establish section markers or other inventory controls.
Mark the beginning and end of each inventory section and identify the specialist responsible for the count to prevent duplication or omission.
Perform the physical inventory.
Record product identification and quantities.
Reconcile questions and apparent discrepancies with VA Pharmacy personnel.
Produce section-level inventory information as sections are completed.
Review applicable inventory information with VA Pharmacy personnel.
Perform required recounts or corrections.
Conduct a final facility-wide walkthrough.
Verify that all applicable sections have been counted.
Remove inventory markers.
Complete the on-site closeout with the designated VA Pharmacy representative.
18. SECTION-LEVEL VERIFICATION
For each inventory section, the Contractor shall provide sufficient inventory information to permit review by the Contractor's representative and VA Pharmacy personnel.
At least one detailed inventory printout or electronic equivalent shall be reviewed for each inventory specialist or counter working on the facility inventory.
VA personnel may request additional printouts, recounts, or supporting information when necessary to validate the inventory.
The Contractor shall correct Contractor-caused errors identified during the on-site verification process before departing the facility whenever practicable.
19. FINAL FACILITY CLOSEOUT
Before leaving a facility, the Contractor shall verify that:
All required sections have been counted.
Required recounts have been completed.
Known discrepancies have been addressed or documented.
Automated equipment data has been incorporated as required.
VA-provided controlled-substance data has been incorporated.
Inventory markers have been removed.
The final inventory status has been reviewed with the designated VA Pharmacy representative.
Any outstanding issue requiring Government action has been documented.
20. INVENTORY REPORT
The Contractor shall provide a separate final inventory report for each pharmacy or facility inventoried.
The Contractor shall submit a separate final inventory report for each pharmacy inventoried within seven (7) calendar days following completion of that pharmacy s physical inventory. All physical inventories at the six facilities must be completed by February 28 of the applicable contract year.
Each report shall contain, at a minimum:
Facility and pharmacy/section.
Item name and strength.
Package description.
NDC, UPC, or UPN, as applicable.
McKesson item number, when applicable.
Quantity.
Current package price.
Extended replacement cost by item.
Section/location.
Total replacement cost for the inventory.
The report shall be provided in an electronic format that can be opened, reviewed, and transferred into Microsoft Excel or an equivalent Government-approved spreadsheet application.
21. REPORT FORMAT
The Contractor shall provide data in a structured electronic format.
The Contractor shall ensure that each inventory item is represented by a discrete record; quantities and prices are numeric and usable for calculation; product identifiers are not truncated or improperly formatted; extended costs can be independently calculated from quantity and unit price; section/location information is retained; and facility and pharmacy totals reconcile to the underlying item-level data.
22. QUALITY CONTROL
The Contractor shall maintain an internal quality-control process sufficient to ensure the accuracy and completeness of each inventory.
The Contractor shall review inventory data for duplicate entries, missing product identifiers, missing quantities, missing prices, incorrect package descriptions, invalid or inconsistent NDC information, arithmetic errors, duplicate sections, omitted sections, and other apparent data-quality problems.
The Contractor shall correct Contractor-caused errors at no additional cost to the Government.
23. GOVERNMENT ACCEPTANCE
The Government will review each deliverable for completeness, accuracy, and compliance with this SOW.
A deliverable may be rejected when it:
Omits required inventory areas or items.
Contains material data inaccuracies.
Contains unsupported or unexplained pricing information.
Does not contain the required data elements.
Contains duplicate or omitted inventory records.
Cannot be reconciled to the physical inventory.
Otherwise fails to meet a material requirement of this SOW.
If a deliverable is rejected because of Contractor-caused deficiencies, the Contractor shall correct and resubmit it within three (3) business days after notification, unless the Contracting Officer establishes a different period.
Contractor-caused corrections shall be completed within three business days of notification unless otherwise directed by the Contracting Officer. The Contracting Officer must confirm contractual handling of corrections requested after the February 28 inventory period.
Corrections required because of Contractor error shall be performed at no additional cost to the Government.
