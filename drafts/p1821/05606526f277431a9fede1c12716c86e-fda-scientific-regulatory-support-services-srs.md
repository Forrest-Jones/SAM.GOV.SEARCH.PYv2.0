<p><strong>Project Title:</strong> FDA Scientific and Regulatory Support Services (SRS) IDIQ</p>

<p><strong>Background/Overview: </strong>The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) is a science-based regulatory agency critically important to the stability of the nation&#39;s public health and health care systems. FDA protects and promotes the health and safety of all Americans by enhancing the availability of safe medical products, foods, and animal products, and by promoting innovation that addresses unmet medical and public health needs.</p>

<p>FDA decisions must be based on the best available scientific data and employ the best tools and methods available to ensure products meet the highest quality standards, while fostering innovation in the products it regulates. In support of its regulatory mission, FDA conducts scientific research across a broad range of disciplines including vaccines, drug safety, food safety, medical devices, biologics, toxicology, radiological products, and more. FDA&#39;s science is increasingly data-centric, complex, and collaborative, involving multidisciplinary groups across its Centers and Offices. Within the Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER), the Office of Computational Science (OCS), established under the Office of Translational Sciences, supports the data management, technical, and analytic needs of the regulatory review process. OCS manages and supports analytical tools, data standards, and computing infrastructure that form the backbone of modern regulatory review for medical products.</p>

<p><strong>Purpose:</strong> The FDA is seeking information from experienced sources in Scientific and Regulatory Support Services as it is contemplating the establishment of a Indefinite Delivery Indefinite Quantity (IDIQ) to such services. The FDA&rsquo;s primary objective for this effect is to obtain Contractor services related to scientific and regulatory support across a broad range of mission requirements. Depending on the requirement, services may support scientific research, regulatory review, clinical and non-clinical analyses, epidemiology and surveillance, scientific data interpretation, laboratory activities, technical writing, training, and scientific program coordination.</p>
