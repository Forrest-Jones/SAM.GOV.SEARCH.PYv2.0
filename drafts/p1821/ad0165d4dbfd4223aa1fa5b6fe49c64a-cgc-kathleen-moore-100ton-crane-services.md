XI: SCOPE OF WORK:<br />
3.1 Pre-Lift Planning<br />
&bull;Develop and submit a Rigging Plan detailing crane type, lifting capacities, load charts, and step-by-step evolution for engine removals.<br />
&bull;Identify all lifting appliances (slings, spreader bars, shackles, etc.) and ensure they are properly rated and certified.<br />
&bull;Provide certification documentation for crane, rigging gear, and operators prior to commencement.<br />
&bull;Incorporate stability considerations and counterbalance weight requirements to maintain safe load handling.<br />
3.2 Crane Operations<br />
&bull;Engine Removal: Using certified lifting gear and spreader bars, remove existing MDEs through designated access openings. Coordinate with Contractor performing engine preservation.<br />
&bull;Engine Installation: Rig and install two Government-furnished MTU 20V4000M93L engines, aligning them within tolerances to reduction gearboxes.<br />
3.3 Safety &amp; Notifications<br />
&bull;Comply with OSHA (29 CFR 1915) and Coast Guard facility safety requirements.<br />
&bull;Provide a spotter/signalman for all critical lifts.<br />
&bull;Notify COR/Port Engineer 24 hours in advance of all lift operations for Government witness and inspection.</p>

<p>4. Deliverables<br />
&bull;Rigging Plan: Detailed lifting sequence and certification documentation.<br />
&bull;Condition Found Reports (CFRs): Submitted for any structural or lifting discrepancies.<br />
&bull;Final Report: Documenting successful completion of engine lifting operations.</p>

<p>5. Schedule &bull; Engine Removal &amp; Installation: During main engine renewal availability.</p>

<p>6. Quality Assurance<br />
All crane operators must hold current certification under OSHA/ANSI standards. Rigging equipment shall be inspected before each lift. All evolutions must be witnessed and signed off by the Contracting Officer&rsquo;s Representative (COR)/Port Engineer.</p>
