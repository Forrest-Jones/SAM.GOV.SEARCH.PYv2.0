Can the Government clarify what constitutes minor repairs included in the fixed-price requirement versus repairs requiring a separate contract modification? Minor repairs are those corrective actions that are incidental to the testing, inspection, and preventive maintenance services specifically identified in the SOW and that can be completed as part of the scheduled maintenance effort without requiring replacement of major equipment or components. Examples include tightening electrical connections, torquing hardware, cleaning, lubrication, adjustment, calibration, minor housekeeping activities, and similar corrective actions identified during testing and inspection. The SOW expressly includes &quot;onsite testing, inspection, minor repair, and housekeeping&quot; as part of the required services. Repairs that exceed basic or minor repair, including major component replacement, equipment refurbishment, or other work outside the defined maintenance and testing scope, shall not be performed under the fixed-price requirement. The contractor shall submit a cost estimate and scope of work to the COR for review and routing to the Contracting Officer (CO). Such work requires CO approval and may require a separate order/contract or contract modification and funding authorization before performance. As stated in the SOW, &quot;In the event faults or repairs identified during maintenance or inspection exceed basic repair, Contractor shall submit cost estimate and Scope of Work to COR for routing to CO for approval and contract modification.
