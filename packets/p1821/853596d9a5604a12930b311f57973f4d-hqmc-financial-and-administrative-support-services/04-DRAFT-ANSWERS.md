## Before you read the answers: a blocker in the PWS

Section 10.1 of the draft PWS:

> "At time of contract award, all of the Contractor's staff assigned to this
> contract shall have and maintain a Secret clearance and the Contractor shall
> have and maintain a **Secret facility clearance**. USMC will sponsor
> reinvestigations, but **will not sponsor initial Secret clearances** for
> Contractor personnel unless this requirement is waived by the COR."

Proverbs 1821 holds neither a facility clearance nor cleared personnel, and a
facility clearance cannot be obtained speculatively — it requires a sponsoring
contract. As drafted, the firm cannot perform this requirement as prime, and a
subcontractor supplying assigned staff would face the same personnel
requirement.

That does not make responding pointless. It makes the response's most valuable
content the answers to questions (c) and (e), where the Government is explicitly
asking what would limit competition. A market research notice is the correct and
only place to say this, and saying it well is worth more than a polished claim
of capability you cannot deliver.

## a. Better NAICS / PSC combination?

> **Draft.** Partially. NAICS 541219 (Other Accounting Services) fits scope area
> (5) Financial Audit Assistance and the financial reporting elements of (2).
> It fits the balance of the requirement less well. Scope areas (1)
> Contract/Project Management Support and (3) Administrative Assistance for
> Senior Leaders are management and administrative support rather than
> accounting services, and on our reading of Section 5.0 they represent the
> majority of the labor.
>
> We would suggest the Government consider NAICS 541611 (Administrative
> Management and General Management Consulting Services) as the primary code,
> with 541219 retained as secondary for the audit assistance tasks. PSC R499
> (Support – Professional: Other) is appropriately broad and we would not
> change it; if the Government prefers a narrower code, R408 (Program
> Management/Support Services) or R710 (Financial Management Services) each
> describe parts of the requirement more precisely than R499 describes all of
> it.
>
> We note that the choice materially affects the competitive pool, because the
> size standard and the set of registered small businesses differ between the
> two codes.

**Note to you:** this recommendation is self-interested — you are registered in
541611 and not in 541219. It also happens to be defensible on the merits. Decide
whether you are comfortable making it, and if you are, make it on the scope
argument and nothing else. Separately, **add 541219 and PSC R499 to your SAM
registration regardless** — the notice states interested parties must be a small
business for the assigned NAICS code to be eligible as prime.

## b. Better contract type?

> **Draft.** The notice anticipates Firm Fixed Price. FFP is well suited to the
> recurring, scheduled deliverables in Section 6.0 — the monthly and quarterly
> reporting, the data calls with defined cadence — where scope and volume are
> predictable.
>
> It is less well suited to the request-driven work. The Written
> Recommendations and Analysis for Specific Issues deliverable (PWS 5.1.1.1,
> 5.1.3.1, 5.4.1 and 5.4.3) is provided "when requested," within 5 to 10
> business days of each request, on subject matter the requestor defines at the
> time. Neither the annual volume of requests nor their individual scope is
> stated. Pricing that firm-fixed requires the contractor to price a
> distribution it cannot see, which in practice produces either a risk premium
> the Government pays for unnecessarily or an underbid that degrades
> responsiveness.
>
> We would suggest a hybrid structure: FFP CLINs for the scheduled deliverables
> and administrative support, and either Time-and-Materials CLINs or a priced
> IDIQ ordering structure for the on-demand analytical work. Publishing the
> historical annual volume of WRASI requests would also allow FFP pricing of
> that element if the Government prefers to keep a single contract type.

## c. Risk areas and concerns, and mitigation

> **Draft.** Three, in order of how much they affect the Government's outcome.
>
> **1. The security requirement will substantially limit competition, and most
> sharply among the small businesses the anticipated set-aside is meant to
> reach.** PWS 10.1 requires a Secret facility clearance and Secret clearances
> for all assigned staff at time of award, while stating the Government will not
> sponsor initial clearances. A facility clearance cannot be obtained without a
> sponsoring contract, so the eligible pool is limited to firms that already hold
> an FCL and already employ cleared financial analysts and administrative staff.
> Combined with the anticipated FAR 52.219-14 limitation on subcontracting,
> which requires the prime to perform at least half the labor with its own
> employees, a compliant bidder must hold both the clearance infrastructure and
> the in-house cleared staff.
>
> Mitigations the Government may wish to consider: sponsoring initial clearances
> for a defined number of key personnel; permitting a phase-in period during
> which cleared staffing is achieved against a milestone schedule; or separating
> the work that PWS 7.1 already contemplates being performed remotely and
> unclassified into CLINs with a lower clearance threshold. Any of the three
> would widen the pool without changing what the Government receives.
>
> **2. Transition continuity.** The requirement is currently performed under
> GS10F0168W, task order M9549422F0021. The institutional knowledge in scope
> areas (2) and (5) — DAI configuration history, the current state of
> Complementary User Entity Control implementation, the reporting lineage behind
> recurring products — is not recoverable from the PWS alone. A priced phase-in
> period with incumbent overlap, and a required turnover of reporting artifacts
> and data dictionaries as a deliverable, would reduce the risk of a reporting
> gap in the first quarter of performance.
>
> **3. Dependency on Government-controlled systems.** Performance depends on
> MCEN-N access via CAC and on the effectiveness of DAI Complementary User
> Entity Controls the contractor does not own. Delay in CAC issuance or account
> provisioning directly delays deliverables. Defining a Government response time
> for access provisioning, and treating delays beyond it as excusable, would
> place the risk with the party that controls it.

## d. Is the PWS an adequate basis for a proposal?

> **Draft.** The PWS is sufficient to understand the scope and to respond to
> this notice. It is not yet sufficient to price a Firm Fixed Price proposal.
> The following would be needed:
>
> - Estimated level of effort by scope area and labor category, or the
>   incumbent's current staffing levels
> - Historical annual volume of the request-driven deliverables, particularly
>   WRASI
> - Period of performance and option structure
> - The number of cleared personnel required, by labor category, and whether
>   any clearance phase-in is permitted
> - Specific place of performance for the onsite portions of PWS 5.2, which
>   determines both the applicable wage determination and the recruiting market
> - The anticipated CLIN structure, and whether transition-in and
>   transition-out are separately priced
>
> The first two are the difference between a priced offer and an estimate.

## e. Unique support requirements not correctly captured in the PWS?

> **Draft.** Three observations.
>
> **Organizational conflict of interest.** Scope area (5) provides assistance to
> the MCNAFAS branch, which performs independent audits of Non-Appropriated Fund
> Instrumentalities. If the same contractor also supports financial operations
> or reporting for entities MCNAFAS audits, whether under this contract or
> another, that is an organizational conflict of interest the PWS does not
> currently address. An explicit OCI clause, and a statement of whether
> audit-assistance staff may work on non-audit tasks, would remove the ambiguity
> before award rather than after.
>
> **Records management and accessibility.** Scope area (3) includes document
> control, correspondence and records support for senior leaders. The PWS does
> not reference the contractor's obligations under Federal records management
> requirements or Section 508 accessibility for the products delivered. Both
> affect how the administrative work is performed and priced.
>
> **Surge.** The PWS sets deliverable turnaround times but does not describe
> expected surge periods. Financial support work of this kind concentrates around
> budget submission and fiscal year end. Identifying those periods would let
> offerors staff for them deliberately rather than absorb them.

## f. Do these services meet the FAR 2.101 definition of a commercial service?

> **Draft.** Substantially yes, with two exceptions.
>
> Financial analysis, budget formulation and execution support, reconciliation,
> management reporting, and executive and administrative staff support are
> services of a type customarily offered and sold competitively in substantial
> quantities in the commercial marketplace, performed under standard commercial
> terms, and are within FAR 2.101(1)(vi) as services of a type offered and sold
> competitively based on established market prices for specific tasks performed.
>
> Two elements are not commercial in nature and are Government-unique: work
> performed within the Defense Agencies Initiative and against DAI Complementary
> User Entity Controls, and the security requirements in PWS 10.0. Those are
> conditions of performance rather than changes to the nature of the service, and
> in our view do not remove the requirement from commercial services treatment.
