# Audit — 11 November 2026

Review of the SAM.gov search tool as it stood on `cursor/daily-top-two-581a`
(the daily shortlist already cut to two), before the winnability changes
on this branch. The question underneath every finding is the same: will
this pick a bid Proverbs 1821 can win, given a small business with no
federal past performance, an enrolled agent and no CPA, no clearance,
and construction capability that is real but local.

Severity is about the chance of spending a week on a notice that cannot
be won, or of never seeing one that could.

## How a notice moves

One Python pipeline, `find_opportunities.py`, serves the CLI, the GitHub
Action, and the website.

1. **Fetch.** For each NAICS code on the active profile, `fetch` pages
   `https://api.sam.gov/opportunities/v2/search`. Results are deduped by
   `noticeId`.
2. **Filter.** Supply buys (digit PSC) drop. Set-asides the firm does
   not hold drop. Deadlines already past drop. Sources sought and
   presolicitations stay in the list.
3. **Score and rank.** `score` adds a handful of points. The list sorts
   by that integer, highest first. Ties keep whatever order the NAICS
   queries happened to return.
4. **Digest and terminal.** `daily_shortlist` keeps the top 2
   (`DEFAULT_DAILY_TOP_N`, `--top`). The email and the Actions log show
   those two. A credential gate can move a notice from the recommendation
   into a "cannot bid" note. It does not promote number 3 into the slot.
5. **Packets.** `--batch N` walks the ranked list and builds N *new*
   packet folders (proposal, price sheet, quote email, attachments).
   That is not the same as "the top N." The morning job asks for 2.
6. **Website.** `webapp/app.py` calls the same `run_search`. Home shows
   two cards. Contracts shows the full ranked list.

State is a sqlite file of notice ids already seen. Drafts and packets
are markdown and CSV in the repo. The scheduled workflow
(`.github/workflows/daily-search.yml`) runs at 10:00 America/Chicago,
with a second cron so daylight saving does not skip a day, and commits
a heartbeat so GitHub does not disable the schedule.

Secrets: `SAM_API_KEY` and the Gmail app password come from the
environment or GitHub Actions secrets. `.env` and `.env.local` are
gitignored. Nothing in this review found a key in the tree.

## Does the score pick winnable work?

Before this branch, a notice's score was:

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Set-aside the firm holds (small business) | +2 |
| Deadline inside the window (default 7–60 days) | +2 |
| Priority PSC | +1 |
| Title keywords, capped | +2 |
| Place of performance in Texas | +1 |

That is a relevance sort, not a win sort. It treats these as equal, and
they are not:

- **Set-aside.** A total small-business set-aside is the firm's actual
  opening. Full and open, and any best-value evaluation that scores
  past performance, favors incumbents and firms with federal history.
  The score gave the set-aside two points and then stopped. It did not
  read LPTA versus best value at all.
- **NAICS fit.** The search covers every code on the registration plus
  the construction trades. `packet_naics` later refuses some packets,
  but the rank that fills the daily two does not. A confined-space
  assessment and a history-office BPA have both shown up for that
  reason.
- **Place of performance.** Texas is one point. A roofing job in
  Colorado or a construction MATOC in Alaska scores almost like the
  same job in Montgomery. For office support that can be done remotely,
  nationwide is fine. For field construction it is not.
- **Days to deadline.** Anything inside the window got the same two
  points. Ten days and fifty days look alike. The date is the first ten
  characters of the timestamp, not a Central Time calendar day, so a
  close just after midnight on the east coast can be a day off.
- **LPTA versus best value.** Not read. For a firm with no federal past
  performance, LPTA is often the winnable case and best value that
  weighs experience is often not.
- **Estimated value.** Not read. A three-thousand-dollar call and a
  job over the Miller Act threshold scored the same. Bonding capacity
  in `company.toml` is still blank.
- **Incumbent and recompete.** Not read. "Follow-on" and "incumbent"
  in the title did not move the score.
- **Past performance.** The fact bank says, correctly, that the firm
  has none. The ranker never used that fact.
- **Licence and clearance.** `packet.py` has credential gates (CPA,
  clearance, PE, RCRA, bonds, OSHA). They run on the description of
  the notices that already made the cut, and they label a notice
  "cannot bid." They did not change the order, so both daily slots
  could be unwinnable while a clean notice sat at number 3. An
  enrolled agent does not satisfy a CPA requirement. The firm has no
  clearance.
- **Site visit.** A mandatory site visit the firm will miss makes the
  quote non-responsive. The ranker did not look for one.

The practical result, visible in the packet tray, is a lot of distant
construction and the occasional professional-services notice the firm
is barred from. The top two were the two highest keyword scores, not
the two most winnable bids.

## Findings

### Critical

**C1. Standalone solicitations were never requested.**
`NOTICE_TYPES` was `p,k,r`: presolicitation, combined synopsis, sources
sought. SAM.gov's code for a solicitation is `o`. Most bids that are
not a commercial-item combined synopsis are type Solicitation. The
morning run could not see them, no matter how well they fit.
*Fixed on this branch:* the search asks for `o,k,p,r`.

**C2. The score cannot tell a winnable bid from a familiar title.**
See the table above. Keyword, deadline, PSC, Texas, and a small-business
set-aside. A Secret clearance, a CPA licence, an LPTA clause, a
mandatory site visit, and an out-of-state roof all passed through that
function untouched.
*Fixed on this branch, for text the tool actually has:* `winnability_adjustment`
adds or subtracts points, with a reason string, for solicitation versus
sources sought, LPTA versus best value that weighs past performance,
clearance, a CPA licence, site visit, incumbent or recompete language,
and field work (NAICS 23 or a Y/Z PSC) outside the home state. The
first score uses the title. The top 12 preliminary leaders are fetched
and scored again with the description, then the list is sorted again,
and only then is the top 2 cut. Twelve description calls stays inside
the 1,000-a-day budget. Notices below that window are not re-read.

**C3. A bad or missing API key, or a date-form Retry-After, failed
obscurely.** `request_with_retry` only retried network errors, 429, and
5xx. HTTP 401 and 403 fell through to `raise_for_status` and killed the
run with a requests traceback. `int(Retry-After)` crashes when SAM.gov
sends an HTTP date, which the header is allowed to be. The missing-key
check lived only in `main`, so the website and any other caller got a
worse message. Description fetches did not retry at all.
*Fixed on this branch:* missing key and 401/403 raise `SamApiError`
with a sentence that says what to do and does not include the key.
Retry-After accepts an integer or an HTTP date, and the wait is capped.
5xx and 429 still back off. A page that fails mid-list is reported as
incomplete instead of being returned as if it were the whole list.
Description fetches use the same client. A 404 on one description does
not abort the run.

### High

**H1. Credential gates do not see the whole list.** Even after this
branch, only the top 12 descriptions are read before the cut. A
clearance requirement that lives only in an attachment, or on notice
number 13, can still outrank a clean notice. Attachment scanning
exists, and it runs while a packet is built, which is after the rank
is frozen.
*Left.* Reading every attachment before ranking will blow the daily
call budget and the runner's time. The next step is to scan
attachments for the finalists only, and to demote a finalist that
fails so the next clean notice can enter the two. That is a behavior
change from the current rule that number 3 does not backfill a blocked
slot, and it should be a deliberate choice.

**H2. Deadline math ignores the clock and the time zone.**
`days_until_deadline` subtracts calendar dates from the first ten
characters of the timestamp. The top-2 card now prints the close in
Central Time, but the score still uses the old day count. A close at
12:30 AM Eastern is still the previous evening in Houston.
*Left* for the score. The card is fixed.

**H3. Same work, two notice ids.** Dedup is `noticeId` only. An
amendment is a new id with the same solicitation number. Both can land
in the top two and both can get a packet.
*Left.*

**H4. Value is displayed and not judged.** The card now shows an
estimated value when the notice states one (or an award amount, if
SAM.gov sent one). A very large construction job and a very small one
still score alike, and blank bonding in the fact bank is only mentioned
when the text itself says bonds are required.
*Left*, on purpose. A dollar figure parsed out of prose is too noisy
to demote a notice automatically. Once `bonding_capacity_single` is
filled in, compare it.

### Medium

**M1. "repair" is a profile keyword.** The module-level `KEYWORDS`
list dropped "repair" because it promoted parts orders. The p1821
profile list still contains it, for roofing and similar titles. It
also matches a lot of work the firm will not travel to do. The new
out-of-state field penalty reduces that. It does not remove it.
*Left.*

**M2. Full and open is not penalized by itself.** An eligible
set-aside still scores higher. A full-and-open notice whose text never
says "best value" or "past performance" is not marked down. Some of
those are LPTA and winnable. A blanket penalty would hide them.
*Left* until the evaluation method is known more often than it is now.

**M3. The website does not show the decision card.** Home still shows
a short card. Contracts still lists every match, which is useful, and
the new reasons do show up in "why this matched" because the website
uses the same score. It does not show Central Time, value, or the risk
lines the email now shows.
*Left.*

**M4. No call budget in the client.** Paging every NAICS code over 90
days can approach the 1,000-request day. The client backs off when it
is told it is rate limited. It does not count calls or pause between
successful pages.
*Left.*

**M5. Two copies of "is this unwinnable."** `packet.credential_gates`
and `winnability_adjustment` both look for clearance and a CPA, with
different expressions. They will drift.
*Left.* One scanner should feed both the score and the packet checklist.

**M6. Incumbent detection is a regex.** USASpending is a free API and
would tell us who holds the predecessor contract. That is a new
integration, not a small patch.
*Left.*

### Low

**L1. Ties are accidental.** Equal scores keep fetch order. Two notices
at the same score should prefer the sooner deadline, then a set-aside
over full and open.
*Left.*

**L2. Tests never saved a real SAM.gov payload.** Set-aside strings in
the tests are real ones observed on a live run, which is good. The HTTP
client had no tests at all until this branch. There is still no
checked-in, redacted search response to pin pagination against.
*Partly fixed:* `tests/test_api.py` covers a missing key, a rejected
key, Retry-After as an integer and as a date, a failed page, and the
`o` ptype. A fixture file is still worth adding.

**L3. CI is one pytest job on Python 3.12.** That matches the runner.
There is no linter. The suite is large and it is about the packet
generator as much as the ranker, which is the right weight for a tool
that writes federal drafts.

**L4. `KEYWORDS` and the profile keyword lists diverge on purpose.**
Not dead code. Easy to "fix" the wrong list. Worth a one-line comment
in the profile, which already exists in spirit at the module list.

**L5. The daily commit identity and `contents: write` are broad.**
Normal for this repo's heartbeat. A scheduled workflow that pushes to
`main` should stay reviewed when the workflow file itself changes.

## What this branch changes

The daily top 2 is unchanged as a rule: two notices, best score first,
`--top` still widens a single run, `--csv` is still the full list.

- The search includes solicitations (`ptype` `o`).
- Leaders are rescored with their descriptions before the cut.
- The score explains LPTA, best value plus past performance, clearance,
  a CPA licence, site visit, incumbent or recompete, solicitation
  versus market research, and out-of-state field work.
- Each of the two picks shows the set-aside, the deadline in Central
  Time, the estimated value or "not stated", the risks (always
  including no federal past performance), and the link.
- The API client fails in sentences, retries with a capped backoff,
  accepts a date-form Retry-After, and does not print the key.

## Roadmap, in the order it earns its keep

1. **Demote a finalist that fails a gate, and let the next clean notice
   into the two.** Today a blocked notice keeps its slot and number 3
   stays off the page. Do this only after the attachment scan runs on
   the finalists, or a clearance buried in a PDF still wins the slot.
2. **One requirements scanner** shared by the score and the packet
   checklist, so CPA, clearance, bonds, and site visit cannot drift.
3. **Deadline and tie-break in Central Time.** Sooner workable deadline
   wins a tie. Day count uses the Houston calendar.
4. **Dedup on solicitation number,** not only notice id, so an
   amendment does not consume both daily slots.
5. **Fill bonding, insurance, and the rate card, then score against
   them.** Until `bonding_capacity_single` and the rate card are real,
   the tool should not invent a number and should not pretend a large
   construction bid is ready to price.
6. **A polite call counter** that stops short of the 1,000-a-day cap
   and says which NAICS codes were not finished.
7. **The same decision card on the website,** on the two home picks.
8. **USASpending lookup for recompetes** once the regex has been wrong
   often enough to justify the extra request.
9. **A redacted real search payload** checked into `tests/fixtures` so
   pagination and new SAM.gov fields fail a test instead of a morning.
